17 U.S.C. §§ 106, 501–506Copyright & unauthorized distribution+

Copyright protects the owner’s rights to reproduce, distribute, and publicly perform or display a work, subject to legal limitations and exceptions. Unauthorized uploads and sales can infringe those rights. Civil remedies can include injunctions and damages; certain willful infringement can also carry criminal penalties when the statutory requirements are met.

Purchasing access to a video does not, by itself, transfer its copyright or grant permission to reproduce it and upload it to other websites.

17 U.S.C. § 512DMCA notice & takedown+

The DMCA provides a notice-and-takedown process for reporting online copyright infringement. Qualifying service providers seeking safe-harbor protection must meet applicable requirements, including responding expeditiously to compliant notices. The process also includes counter-notice procedures.

A complete notice identifies the copyrighted work and the infringing location and includes the required contact details, signature, and statements. This general website statement does not replace an individual takedown notice.

PUBLIC LAW 119–12 · SECTION 3TAKE IT DOWN Act+

For requests that qualify under the Act, covered platforms must remove the reported intimate depiction as soon as possible and no later than 48 hours after receiving a valid request, and make reasonable efforts to identify and remove known identical copies. The FTC enforces these platform obligations.

The platform-removal process and the Act’s criminal provisions have different requirements. Whether a particular image or service is covered depends on the law and the facts; this is not a claim that every repost or viewer commits a crime under the Act.
