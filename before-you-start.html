A LETTER TO ANYONE CONSIDERING THE SEX WORK INDUSTRY

Before you start
making porn,
please read this.

If you’re researching how to start making porn, please read this before you decide. I was trying to survive—to keep a roof over our heads and afford diapers and food for my baby. I wish someone had told me what I’m about to tell you before I entered the sex work industry.

01 / THE PRESSURE

I needed diapers.
I needed food.
I was trying to survive.

I remember being the person desperately looking for a way to make ends meet. I was trying not to get evicted. I needed diapers and food for my baby. I had no college degree, I felt pretty hopeless, and I wanted a way to work from home.

If that is where you are right now, I understand why you’re looking. I remember how urgent everything felt. I remember needing an answer that would work before the next bill came due.

At first, I wasn’t going to do anything nude or super sexual. I thought I had made a hard boundary. I thought I knew exactly what I would and wouldn’t do.

02 / THE BOUNDARIES

Then the bills
kept coming.

Once you’re in, and you’re not making money, and the bills are still piling up, that boundary can start to feel very different. The eviction feels closer. Someone offers you money to do something you normally would never have done. And now you’re weighing that offer against what your baby needs.

That is a vulnerable place to make a decision. It is the part I wish I had understood before I started: saying “I would never do that” is easier when you are not desperate.

I grew up sheltered, with strict rules in a religious household. Making porn felt liberating at first. I rationalized it as taking back my power, becoming a new person, deciding what to do with my own body.

What I failed to see was the future I was creating for myself, long after I would set down the camera and walk away.

What felt liberating then became my humiliation and deepest regret ten years later.

03 / THE FUTURE I COULDN’T SEE

You might not always
feel the way you do today.

I think that would have been the hardest thing for my past self to understand. I could imagine making money. I could imagine working from home. I could not really imagine being a different version of myself, years later, wishing those videos did not exist.

Working from home was nice. Making money felt good. There is no guarantee that you will make enough to live on. I eventually gained an audience through the unusual work I made and some luck with celebrity recognition and promotion. Looking back, that attention only made what I’m dealing with today harder.

Walking away from the income can be hard. Walking away from the copies is another struggle entirely. You can stop making content and close your accounts. That does not mean everyone who copied your work will delete it, or that strangers will stop uploading it.

This is not everyone’s story. Some people feel good about their decision. They have made peace with it and have no regrets. I’m not one of them.

You cannot know with certainty how your future self will feel. You do not have to take this risk to earn the right to decide against it.

PLEASE THINK BEYOND RIGHT NOW

What if these vulnerable videos are still circulating in ten, twenty, or thirty years?

What if people you know find them, send them to each other, and laugh at you?

What if your family finds out? What if someone sends them to a future employer specifically to humiliate you?

What if someone you don’t like—someone who won’t leave you alone—finds your videos? What if you know he’s touching himself to your naked body, and you desperately wish he wouldn’t? You can demand removal of unauthorized uploads, but you cannot reach through his screen and make him stop.

Will it still feel worth it?

These are possibilities I wish I had taken seriously. If someone harasses or humiliates you, their behavior is their responsibility. But knowing that does not take away the hurt or put every copy back under your control.

04 / WHAT IT COST ME

I made money.
I still wish I hadn’t done it.

I used my income from sex work to get out of debt, buy a house, and go to college to finish my degree. I’m telling you that because I don’t want you to dismiss this as a story about someone who simply never made it work financially.

I did make money. I did accomplish things. And I am still telling you: for me, it wasn’t worth it.

I don’t have that house anymore. I went back into debt. I thankfully still have my degree, but I wish I had found a different way to get there. The money did not buy me the ability to erase the videos afterward.

Now I live with the fear that my past could be dragged into my present at any moment. I feel like I always have to look behind me to make sure it isn’t getting too close.

And I am angry. Angry that strangers can keep profiting from intimate videos of me while I spend my time trying to get them removed. Angry that I can leave, say no, ask for it to stop, and still have to fight the same fight again.

Some people will not care that you were scared of eviction or trying to feed your baby. They will reduce your whole life to “a whore who got paid.” That cruelty is not the truth about you. It is the judgment I wish I had understood I might have to face long after the money was gone.

There are reporting processes and legal avenues, and I am using them. But do not make this decision assuming that a takedown request or a lawsuit will let you undo it later. Getting unauthorized copies removed can be exhausting, and no one can promise to erase every copy.

05 / WHAT I WANT YOU TO HEAR

My advice?
Don’t start.

If I could speak to the version of myself researching how to get into the sex work industry, I would tell her to walk away. Please don’t do this. Please look for another way.

I wish I had known more about community resources. Looking back, I would rather have explored moving somewhere I could get better support, or found another way to work toward the life I wanted. I wish I had understood that the situation I was trying to escape could change, while these recordings could stay with me.

I know “find another way” can sound painfully inadequate when you need money now. I was there. I am not judging the desperation. I am asking you to take seriously what a decision made in that desperation could cost your future self.

Please don’t assume no one will ever find out. Please don’t assume that being comfortable with it today means you will be comfortable with it forever. Please don’t assume that making enough money will make the consequences feel worth it.

I cannot decide for you, and I cannot speak for every performer. But you came looking for another person’s experience. This is mine, and I don’t want to soften it into something I don’t mean.

No amount of money I made was worth what I am living with now.
I wish I had never started.

Leena Mae
