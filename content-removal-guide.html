FOR SEX WORKERS AND CURRENT AND FORMER ADULT CONTENT CREATORS

Found stolen content?
Start here.

An easy-to-follow content removal guide for sex workers, adult performers, and people who have left the sex work industry. Learn how to report stolen adult videos and unauthorized intimate images. You do not have to solve the whole internet today. Start with one link, one report, and a record of what happens.

IF YOU ONLY HAVE ENERGY FOR ONE THING

Save the exact page address and use that website’s official intimate-image or copyright reporting process, choosing the route that fits. Save the confirmation. You can come back to the rest later.

This is general information, with U.S. reporting routes and some international tools—not legal advice or a removal service. Policies and eligibility vary. No method can promise to erase every copy. You deserve support whether you still perform, have left, or never worked in the industry.

Were you under 18 when the image was made? Use NCMEC’s Take It Down, even if you are an adult now. Do not download or share sexual images of a minor to make a report. Use files already on your device for its hashing tool. This is different from the FTC reporting site discussed below.

01 / SAVE THE LINKS

Make a small record.
Not a new ordeal.

  1. Copy the full address of each page showing your content, rather than only the homepage or search results.
  2. Note the website name, date found, and what remains: video, download link, preview, thumbnail, or listing.
  3. If a normal download or player link reveals a different hosting website, save that exact source address too. Do not buy access, install software, or download a video just to identify it. If you cannot reach the source, report the visible page anyway.
  4. Keep reports, replies, case numbers and check dates together. Ask someone you trust to help if viewing the pages is distressing.

Download a blank removal tracker (CSV). It contains headings only; keep your completed copy private.

What can help establish ownership?

Keep original files, project files, dated publication or sales records, and relevant contracts securely. Watermarks can support your account, but do not settle every ownership question. ID can help verify who you are; by itself it does not prove copyright ownership.

Do not publish intimate originals or identity documents as proof. If a provider asks for verification, check the recipient and ask what is necessary, how it is protected, and whether a less revealing alternative works. A separate reporting email can help organize correspondence, but does not guarantee anonymity.

02 / CHOOSE THE RIGHT ROUTE

Consent and copyright
are different questions.

If this describes youStart with
You are depicted in intimate content shared without your consent.The service’s nonconsensual intimate imagery (NCII), privacy, or intimate-image removal process. You do not need to claim copyright ownership to explain that you are the person depicted.
You own the copyright, or are authorized to act for its owner.The service’s copyright/DMCA process. This can also cover non-explicit works, where the use infringes copyright.
Both apply.State both grounds truthfully. Use separate forms if the service requires them.
A studio or someone else may own the rights, or a contract may authorize distribution.Review the agreement and seek legal help with disputed permissions. Do not certify ownership you do not have.

Creating or selling content does not grant everyone permission to redistribute it. But retiring or changing your mind does not automatically cancel a previously valid license. Describe the specific upload and why it is unauthorized; do not assume every copy is legally identical.

What a U.S. DMCA notice needs

A notice identifies the work, exact infringing locations, your contact information and signature. It also includes good-faith and accuracy/authority statements. Use the official form or the U.S. Copyright Office’s requirements and sample notice; sign only statements that are true. Consider whether the use is authorized by law, including fair use.

Notices may be forwarded. Review privacy terms and ask about an authorized representative if disclosure is a concern. A counter-notice can lead to restoration; seek legal help promptly if you receive one. Copyright reporting is not a guaranteed permanent removal.

03 / REPORT EACH PLACE THAT CONTROLS THE CONTENT

The page and the file
may live in different places.

Look for “Report,” “Abuse,” “Privacy,” “Take It Down,” or “DMCA” on the site. Use its current official reporting channel. If the page links to a file-sharing service, report the exact file there as well.

Ask for removal of the unauthorized material, including related previews and thumbnails under the recipient’s control. List image addresses when available. If only the download disappears, follow up about remaining intimate images and the listing. A bare text page may require a different basis for removal; explain what it still displays or links to.

A plain-language starting message
Subject: Unauthorized intimate content — removal request I am the person depicted in the intimate material at the URLs below. I did not authorize this distribution. Exact page URLs: [insert] Source file or image URLs, if available: [insert] Material identifying me and enough detail to locate it: [describe briefly] Please remove the reported material and associated unauthorized intimate previews or thumbnails under your control. Please address known identical copies where applicable. Please confirm the action taken for each URL and provide a case reference. Please explain any additional information needed and how it can be supplied securely. [Your name/signature and required contact details]

Adapt this to the facts. Complete the provider’s required fields, declarations and signature. This starter is not a complete DMCA notice and does not replace a statutory or platform-specific form.

If ordinary reporting stalls, the optional advanced reporting section explains hosting-provider escalation, Cloudflare, repeat-upload evidence and federal reporting.

04 / REDUCE SEARCH VISIBILITY

Make it harder to find.
Keep pursuing the source.

Google’s personal sexual-content removal guidance explains eligibility and its request process. Its rules include limits involving consensually created commercial content; do not assume every performer’s work qualifies. Copyright owners can also consider its legal copyright-removal route.

Search removal does not delete the original website or file. If the source has already changed but the search result is outdated, use Google’s linked outdated-content refresh process. Report exact result/page addresses, and keep source removal and search removal as separate tasks.

A TOOL THAT MAY HELP WITH RE-SHARING

StopNCII.org creates a digital fingerprint from eligible adult intimate images on your device; the image itself stays there. Participating platforms use the fingerprint to review matching uploads under their policies. Check eligibility, keep your case details, and understand it does not cover the whole internet or guarantee every edited copy will match.

05 / CHECK WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGED

A receipt is a start.
A removal is a separate result.

Record “submitted,” “provider says removed,” and “checked unavailable” separately. Recheck the exact addresses without downloading media. A timeout, login wall or browser error does not prove deletion. Check the page, images and source file separately; update the same case with the precise material still available.

For platforms covered by the U.S. TAKE IT DOWN Act, the law requires a reporting process and removal of qualifying intimate imagery within 48 hours of a valid request, with reasonable efforts to identify and remove known identical copies. The clock is not a universal deadline for every copyright complaint.

If a covered platform lacks the process or fails to act on a valid request, report it to the FTC. Keep the original notice, receipt and dates. The FTC uses reports for enforcement; filing does not promise an individual investigation or immediate deletion. See the FTC’s current guidance.

06 / YOU DO NOT HAVE TO DO THIS ALONE

Get help with the work.
And with what it costs you.

The Cyber Civil Rights Initiative Safety Center offers support resources for people facing intimate-image abuse. Use its current information to find an appropriate starting point, including when you need help understanding your options.

If there are threats, extortion, stalking or an immediate safety risk, seek appropriate local support or law enforcement as well as content removal. Preserve threatening messages privately. Removal reports and criminal reports serve different purposes.

You are allowed to put this down and come back. You do not have to find every copy today. A trusted person helping with a few links, or one properly documented report, can be a starting point.

Your work does not erase your boundaries.
Your need for help is enough.

OPTIONAL / ADVANCED REPORTING

Still have some fight in you?
Make the record hard to ignore.

If someone is profiting from your stolen sexual content, you are allowed to be furious. My version of being a pain in a pirate’s ass is documenting what they are doing and putting that evidence in front of the companies and authorities that can act. A dead download link does not have to be the end of your report when unauthorized intimate previews are still sitting there.

This section is for when you have the energy. You do not owe anyone an investigation. The aim is removal and appropriate enforcement based on evidence—not flooding inboxes or treating suspicion as proof.

1. Follow the file: report the page, host and images separately

A piracy listing may use one company for the website, another for downloadable videos, and another for previews. Save the exact addresses shown through ordinary page links or player controls. Right-clicking a visible image and choosing “Copy image address,” when available, can identify a preview’s separate address without saving the picture.

Group your inventory by provider. Send each company the URLs it controls, paired with the pages displaying them. Ask for removal of unauthorized files, previews, thumbnails and cached variants under its control. Request review of repeat-uploading accounts and duplicate-prevention measures where supported. Do not claim a text-only listing itself contains intimate imagery.

Keep the original URL even if it redirects. Record the destination separately. Do not guess hidden filenames or bypass a login or paywall; a visible listing is still evidence you can report.

2. Use Cloudflare to pursue the infrastructure trail

When a site uses Cloudflare, its nonconsensual sexually explicit imagery form can be an additional reporting route for qualifying material. Complete its actual attestations truthfully. For non-explicit copyrighted content, choose the relevant copyright route instead.

Cloudflare may be a security/CDN intermediary rather than the host. Ask whether it can identify or notify the responsible host and retain the case number. Follow up with that host through its verified abuse channel. Cloudflare’s published approach explains why its ability to act depends on the service involved. A Cloudflare IP address does not establish that it stores the original files.

Check forwarding and contact-sharing settings before submitting. A request for confidential treatment is worthwhile, but it is not a promise that your information will stay private.

3. Tell the registrar and host apart

ICANN Lookup can help identify a domain’s registrar and published registration information. A registrar manages a domain registration; a host stores or serves the website. Nameservers and an IP address can provide leads, but a proxy or shared infrastructure can hide the actual host.

Verify a provider’s role before accusing it of hosting the material. Read its published abuse policy and use the relevant route. Registrar escalation may be appropriate for a documented violation within its remit, but it is not a substitute for reporting the content to its host. Do not frame ordinary piracy as malware or phishing to get a different department’s attention.

If you are stuck, ask a trusted technical helper to map the publicly visible provider information. There is no need to expose your story publicly to ask for that help.

4. Make repeat behavior visible

Build a short timeline: exact URL, date found, notice sent, acknowledgment, result, and later changes. Preserve original emails and case numbers. An unchanged listing date or a new date in a title does not prove a reupload.

If a file was confirmed removed and a new copy appears, document both URLs and both checks. Ask the provider to review the associated accounts under its repeat-infringement or abuse policies. Include prior case references instead of restarting with an unsupported accusation.

Be precise: “The file is unavailable, but these six preview-image URLs remain accessible” gives a reviewer something concrete to act on. Ask for a human review when a response does not address the actual material reported.

5. Document the business side without paying the site

Public subscription offers, purchase pages and named merchant services can help document how a site presents or monetizes the material. Record what you can see without making a purchase. A card logo alone does not prove which processor handles payments.

If you can verify an involved payment or advertising provider, check its official acceptable-use and abuse procedures. Submit the relevant URLs and evidence of the relationship; request a compliance review. Ask about confidentiality before sending sensitive evidence. Never invent a transaction, impersonate a customer, or file a chargeback for a purchase you did not make.

If the reporting route is inaccessible or demands unnecessary exposure, a separate process complaint can ask the company’s compliance or privacy team for a safer route. Explain the obstacle and the change you need. Payment action is not guaranteed and would not itself erase the files.

6. Prepare an evidence packet for the FBI or National IPR Center

The FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) accepts reports of suspected cyber-enabled crime. The National Intellectual Property Rights Coordination Center accepts referrals about suspected intellectual-property violations, including piracy. These are reporting channels, not emergency removal services or promises of prosecution.

  1. Start with a short account. What was distributed, why you say it was unauthorized, when you discovered it, and how it affected you.
  2. Attach or retain a clear URL inventory as the form permits. Separate listing pages, hosted files and images. Identify each provider’s documented role; leave unknown operators unknown.
  3. Explain your evidence. Describe originals, publication records and relevant agreements you retain. Being depicted and owning copyright are separate facts.
  4. Summarize prior notices and responses. Include dates, case references, removals, refusals and remaining material. Successful removal still belongs in the historical record.
  5. Describe harm accurately. Explain emotional impact and any documented financial loss. If no loss is quantified, say so; do not estimate revenue or damages without a basis.
  6. Preserve originals privately. Offer to provide additional evidence through an appropriate secure channel. Do not upload your entire inbox, ID collection or intimate videos unless specifically required through a verified process.

Read each form’s privacy notice and declarations. Save the submitted narrative and confirmation. Refer to earlier report numbers when providing new information through the agency’s current process. Correct mistakes openly. Neither referral guarantees an investigation or response.

A FIRM FOLLOW-UP YOU CAN ADAPT

Re: [case reference] — material remains accessible On [date], I reported [exact URLs]. Your reply stated [accurate summary]. On [check date], the following material remained available: [URLs and what each displays]. Please review these specific remaining items, including unauthorized previews and thumbnails under your control. Please confirm the action for each item and review documented repeat uploads under your applicable policies. If this material is outside your control, please identify the responsible provider if known, or direct me to the appropriate reporting route.

The point is to make the evidence clear enough to follow and difficult to dismiss. You can be angry, persistent, and exact. You do not need to threaten anyone or exaggerate what happened to deserve action.
