TO THOSE WHO HAVE LEFT THE SEX WORK INDUSTRY

You left.
You’re not alone.

I don’t know your story or what brought you here. But somehow you found this page, and now you’re reading it. If you’re trying to move on while the past keeps following you, this letter is for you.

I’m not on the other side yet.
I’m here with you.

01 / THE LONELINESS

I don’t know your story.
But I know this struggle.

I can’t know your personal experience. I don’t know what you went through, what you’re going through now, why you got into the sex work industry, or why you left.

But I understand the struggle of trying to move on with your life when the past is always following right behind you, never really letting you go fully.

I know it can be a dark place. I know it can feel lonely, like you can’t talk about it with anyone. Like explaining what hurts would mean telling someone something you’re afraid they will use to judge you.

I understand that very well.

You don’t have to have the same regrets as me to belong here. You don’t have to explain why you started or prove that your reasons for leaving were good enough. If you’re struggling with what keeps following you, I want you to know that someone else understands.

02 / THE ANGER

I understand
the anger, too.

The anger at people who are cruel to you for a laugh with their friends. They get a joke, and you get to be the punchline. They go on with their day, and you’re left carrying the humiliation.

The anger at your past self for making the decision in the first place. And then the sadness for that same person, because you remember how scared or desperate you were. You remember feeling like there was no other way.

It is hard to be angry at someone you also want to reach back in time and help.

The anger at pirates who upload your content without permission and make money from your body and your vulnerability. The anger of finding another collection, another website, another copy of something you have already tried so hard to get taken down.

The anger at having to ask again. Explain again. Prove yourself again. While someone else keeps uploading.

You are allowed to be angry.
None of this should be happening to you.

03 / THE COPIES THAT KEEP COMING

I left.
Why won’t they let me leave?

I left. I took down my accounts. I deleted my videos. I did not give these people permission to upload them to other websites.

And yet there are videos of my naked body on sites I never authorized, available to people I never intended to reach. I want to beg the people uploading them: please stop. Please let me move on.

But the uploads keep coming. It feels like what my body can do for them matters more than what this is doing to me.

I struggle to find words strong enough for how violating it feels when someone else profits from sexual images of your body while you keep saying, “No. Please stop.” Your body becomes something strangers trade and sell, while your wishes are treated as an inconvenience. To me, that feels like exploitation through a screen.

I am angry at how hard it is to get help. Angry at laws and systems that do not feel adequate to what is happening. Angry at companies that make removal so difficult, and at uploaders who seem able to keep doing it without consequences.

It can feel as though once something is called porn, people stop asking whether it was stolen, whether the person agreed to that upload, or whether someone is being hurt.

There can be thousands of views on a copy you never authorized. Every one of those views is happening while you are trying to put that part of your life behind you. And you’re the one spending your time and energy trying to make it stop.

That exhaustion is real. So is the anger.

04 / YOU DON’T DESERVE THIS

You did not give up
your right to be a person.

Making porn does not mean you deserve to be stripped of dignity, privacy, or a say in your own life. Having been paid does not make you public property.

People seem to miss that part. They talk about what you chose then as if it means they never have to listen to what you are saying now.

Having agreed to create or sell content does not mean you agreed to every unauthorized copy, upload, or sale. And even beyond what a particular law or contract says, your pain deserves to be taken seriously.

I want a world where “I left, and I don’t want this happening to me” matters. Where a person’s wish to move forward carries more weight than a stranger’s desire to keep profiting from them.

You don’t deserve to be mocked and humiliated by people who think they are better than you because they never made porn. You don’t deserve harassment. You don’t deserve to have intimate material used against you.

You don’t have to spend the rest of your life being punished for a decision you made in the past. Whether you regret making the content or simply want control over where it appears, you deserve to be heard.

05 / THE FUTURE I HOPE FOR YOU

I’m not there yet.
But I hope we both get there.

I don’t know what it feels like to have fully moved on. I’m not there yet. I still get angry. I still get exhausted. I still hope there will be a day when the past doesn’t feel so close behind me.

But I hope you make it. I hope you build a beautiful future for yourself. I hope you find success in something you feel proud of. I hope you get to live your life without constantly feeling haunted.

You deserve happiness. You deserve a new chapter. You deserve to be known for more than the things strangers can find about your past.

It makes me angry that we have to fight so hard for that. But leaving took strength. We can keep moving forward, even when it happens slowly.

And moving forward can include taking a break. You do not have to spend every day searching for copies or looking at things that hurt you. Resting is not surrendering. Neither is asking someone you trust to help carry some of this.

Never give up on your future.
You are still here. Your story is still yours.

You have a unique story. I hope the life ahead of you is more beautiful than you can imagine right now.

Leena Mae
